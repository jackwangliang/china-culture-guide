# 03 — Starting Mandarin From Zero

Use this reference for "how do I learn Chinese", tones, characters, apps, HSK, study plans and language partners. The job is to reduce overwhelm: **recommend at most three tools at once** and give a plan that fits in 20 minutes a day.

## Start with the goal, not the app

| Goal | What to prioritise | Realistic first milestone |
|---|---|---|
| Survive a two-week trip | Pinyin, numbers, food, directions, payment phrases | 4 weeks: order a dish and ask the price |
| Chat with a Chinese friend | Tones, high-frequency spoken chunks, typing pinyin | 8–12 weeks: a 10-minute text exchange without a translator |
| Read posts on Xiaohongshu / WeChat | Character recognition, radicals, graded reading | 12 weeks: understand the gist of a short lifestyle post |
| Formal study or exams | Structured course + HSK vocabulary | Follow the current official syllabus |

Ask the goal first. If the user does not know, assume "chat with a friend and read simple posts" — it keeps motivation highest.

## What is actually hard, and what is surprisingly easy

**Hard:** four tones plus a neutral tone; thousands of characters; very few words that resemble English; listening speed in real conversation.

**Easy:** no verb conjugation, no grammatical gender, no plural endings, no articles, no case system, and basic word order is close to English (我喝茶 / wǒ hē chá = "I drink tea"). Grammar is mostly word order and small particles (了 / le, 吗 / ma, 的 / de).

Say this explicitly. Beginners quit because they expect Spanish-with-characters; the real difficulty is front-loaded in tones and characters, then flattens out.

## A minimal tool stack

Choose **one** from each row. A beginner should install at most three apps total. All names below are widely used, freely available in some form, and available on iOS/Android; features and pricing change, so verify in the app store.

| Row | Options | What it is for |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Main course | **HelloChinese** (structured, tone feedback, stroke order); **ChineseSkill** (game-like); **Duolingo** (easy habit, weaker grammar); **LingoDeer** (detailed grammar explanations) | One daily lesson path |
| 2. Dictionary | **Pleco** (handwriting and camera OCR, flashcards); plus **Anki** or **Skritter** later if character writing matters | Looking up anything, anywhere |
| 3. Real input & people | **DuChinese** (graded readers with pinyin/audio); **ChinesePod** (levelled podcasts); **HelloTalk** / **Tandem** (language exchange) | Moving from exercises to real Chinese |

Widely used free or low-cost reference sites: dictionary and stroke-order lookups, HSK vocabulary lists, and graded readers. Do not send beginners to raw newspapers, un-subtitled dramas or classical texts in week one.

## The 20-minute daily routine

1. **5 min — tones and pinyin.** Listen, repeat, record yourself, compare. Practise tone pairs (mā má mǎ mà) and common two-syllable combinations, not isolated words.
2. **10 min — one lesson** in the main course app. Say every line out loud.
3. **3 min — characters.** Learn 3–5 characters through radicals (部首 / bùshǒu), not by drawing them 30 times. Example: 氵 (water) appears in 河, 海, 洗.
4. **2 min — one real thing.** Look up a word seen that day (a dish name, a platform name), or send one short message to a language partner.

**Four-week milestone:** 150–200 recognised words, comfortable with pinyin typing, able to greet, order, thank, refuse and ask "what does this mean?" (这是什么意思？/ zhè shì shénme yìsi?).

## Tones, characters and typing — the practical order

1. **Pinyin (拼音)** first, with tones marked. It is a pronunciation tool, not a replacement for characters.
2. **Characters (汉字 / Hànzì)** through radicals and components. Recognising 200–300 high-frequency characters unlocks a surprising amount of signage and menus.
3. **Typing before handwriting.** Everyone types pinyin on a phone; handwriting is optional for most learners. Install the pinyin keyboard and use it from week one.
4. **Handwriting** only if the learner enjoys it — then use stroke-order animation (Skritter or a stroke-order dictionary).

## Exams and levels

HSK (汉语水平考试 / Hànyǔ Shuǐpíng Kǎoshì) is the standard proficiency test, used for university admission and some job applications. Its levels and vocabulary lists have been revised in recent years, so **check the current syllabus at the official Chinese Testing International site (chinesetest.cn)** rather than relying on an old "HSK 1–6" word list from a blog. CEFR-style comparisons are approximate.

Do not push exams on a learner whose goal is making friends.

## Learning from real Chinese content

Once week 3–4 arrives, use the platforms this skill covers elsewhere:

- **WeChat 搜一搜** for long articles on a hobby — see `05-wechat-search-and-accounts.md`.
- **Xiaohongshu / RedNote** for short photo-and-text posts: short sentences, lots of visual context, ideal reading practice.
- **Bilibili** for subtitled video; slow the playback to 0.75× and turn on Chinese subtitles.
- **Method:** pick one post, write down three words you can guess from context, look them up in Pleco, and reuse one in a comment. Ten minutes of this beats an hour of passive scrolling.

## Finding a language partner respectfully

Language exchange (语伴 / yǔbàn) works when it is genuinely mutual. See `04-making-friends.md` for platform and etiquette details.

- State your level and goal in the first message, in both languages.
- Split time evenly (e.g. 10 minutes Chinese, 10 minutes English) and use a timer.
- Ask for correction on one thing only ("please correct my tones") rather than every message.
- Do not treat a partner as a free translator for homework, documents or business emails.
- Useful opener: 我们可以互相练习吗？/ wǒmen kěyǐ hùxiāng liànxí ma? = "Can we practise with each other?"

## Five traps to warn about

1. Collecting apps instead of finishing one course.
2. Studying characters silently and never speaking — tones then fossilise.
3. Translating word-for-word from English; learn chunks (没关系, 不好意思, 随便) as units.
4. Using only textbook dialogues and then being unable to read a real menu or post.
5. Assuming a Chinese friend wants to be a full-time teacher. Keep the friendship and the study separate.
