# 04 — Meeting Chinese People and Making Friends

Use this reference for "how do I make Chinese friends", WeChat etiquette, opening messages, language partners, group behaviour and staying safe. The most useful non-obvious fact: **WeChat is a private network, not a discovery network** — you generally need a QR code, an ID or a shared group before you can talk to someone. Point beginners to QQ groups, Xiaohongshu, Bilibili or a language-exchange app for the *first* contact, then WeChat for the ongoing conversation.

## Where people actually meet

| Place | Chinese | Best for | How first contact happens |
|---|---|---|---|
| Xiaohongshu / RedNote | 小红书 | Everyday life, food, fashion, travel, language exchange | Comment on a post, reply to a comment, post a note |
| QQ groups | QQ群 | Interest communities, study, gaming, fandom, students | Public group search, group number, QR invite — see `06-qq-groups.md` |
| Bilibili | 哔哩哔哩 / B站 | Long video, hobbies, subtitled learning content | Comments, 弹幕 (dànmù, on-screen bullet comments), fan groups |
| Language exchange apps | 语伴 App (HelloTalk, Tandem) | One-to-one practice | Match by language and interest |
| WeChat | 微信 | Keeping in touch with people you already met | QR code (二维码 / èrwéimǎ), WeChat ID, or an invite to a group |
| Weibo / Zhihu | 微博 / 知乎 | Public discussion, Q&A | Replies and comments |
| Offline | 线下活动 | Language corners, board-game cafés, hiking, university clubs | 英语角 (yīngyǔ jiǎo, English corner), Meetup-style events, 桌游 (board games) |

Since early 2025, large numbers of non-Chinese users joined RedNote and were welcomed with everyday conversation, pet photos and language help. That makes it the lowest-friction starting point for a foreigner, but it is still a public platform: be a good guest, read the room, and do not treat every friendly reply as a close friendship.

## A realistic friendship ladder

1. **Public reply** — a comment on a post about a shared interest.
2. **Short direct exchange** — a few messages about that interest.
3. **Regular chat** — add on WeChat or keep the app thread going.
4. **Group** — join a small interest or language-exchange group.
5. **Offline, if ever** — a public place, daytime, with your own transport home.

Skipping straight from step 1 to "let's meet / send me your number" reads as suspicious. Say this gently; it saves beginners a lot of awkwardness.

## First-contact etiquette that actually matters

- **Names**: Chinese names put the family name first (李 / Lǐ in 李明 / Lǐ Míng). Start with the family name plus a friendly title — 李老师 (Teacher Li), 同学 (tóngxué, classmate), or simply 你好 (nǐ hǎo) — and let the other person suggest a first-name basis. Using 亲 (qīn, "dear") is e-commerce style, not for new acquaintances.
- **Why, then what**: one sentence on how you found them, one on the shared interest, one small question. Three short lines beat a long self-introduction.
- **Voice messages**: many people dislike long voice notes from someone they barely know, especially across time zones. Ask 方便发语音吗？/ fāngbiàn fā yǔyīn ma? ("Is voice OK?") first.
- **Stickers (表情包 / biǎoqíngbāo)**: common and warm among friends; keep them neutral at first.
- **Reply pace**: slow replies usually mean busy, not rejection. Avoid double- and triple-messaging.
- **Moments (朋友圈 / péngyouquān)**: semi-private; do not repost someone's photos elsewhere without asking.
- **Compliments**: 哪里哪里 / nǎlǐ nǎlǐ is the traditional deflection; as a foreigner, simply 谢谢 works.
- **Warm phrases vs firm plans**: 有空一起吃饭 / yǒu kòng yìqǐ chīfàn ("let's eat sometime") is often a friendly closing, not a booked invitation. Confirm a day and time before treating it as a plan.

## Opening messages

Full templates with pinyin are in `09-phrasebook.md`. The reliable structure:

> Greeting → where you found them → shared interest → one small, easy-to-answer question → thanks.

Example (language exchange):

> 你好！我看到你也在学英语。我是来自巴西的初学者，正在学中文。我们可以互相练习吗？谢谢！
> Nǐ hǎo! Wǒ kàn dào nǐ yě zài xué Yīngyǔ. Wǒ shì láizì Bāxī de chūxuézhě, zhèngzài xué Zhōngwén. Wǒmen kěyǐ hùxiāng liànxí ma? Xièxie!
> "Hi! I saw you're learning English too. I'm a beginner from Brazil learning Chinese. Could we practise with each other? Thanks!"

Adjust the country and interest; never paste a generic template unchanged to ten people.

## Being a good language partner

See `03-learning-chinese.md` for the study side. Socially: split time fairly, agree on correction style, do not use a partner as a free translator, and keep some conversations purely friendly rather than turning every message into a lesson.

## Boundaries, politely

If a conversation becomes uncomfortable, a short line is enough; no elaborate excuse is required:

> 不好意思，我不太方便。/ bù hǎoyìsi, wǒ bù tài fāngbiàn. = "Sorry, that's not convenient for me."

It is completely fine to keep conversations to food, study, hobbies, travel and daily life. Change the subject or stop replying if pushed.

## Safety checklist (state this for every "make friends" answer)

- Never share passport photos, bank details, one-time SMS codes, home address, or your WeChat/QQ login with an online acquaintance.
- Never send money, invest on someone's recommendation, or join "guaranteed profit", 刷单 (fake-order job), 代购 (paid purchasing) or exam-proxy schemes. Romance-plus-investment approaches (often called 杀猪盘 / shāzhūpán) specifically target language-exchange and friendship contexts.
- Do not scan QR codes or open links from strangers claiming to be official support.
- Meet only in public places, tell someone where you are going, and arrange your own transport.
- Do not hand over your account for "verification help". See `08-getting-set-up.md` for the legitimate verification path.
- Keep platform rules: no mass adds, no advertising, no unsolicited DMs to a whole group.

## Common misunderstandings

| What a beginner thinks | More likely reading |
|---|---|
| "They added me, so we're close friends now" | Adding on WeChat is as casual as following someone |
| "They haven't replied in two days, they're upset" | Time zones, work and study schedules are the usual reason |
| "They insisted on paying, I owe them something big" | Reciprocity is long-term; a small treat next time is enough |
| "Everyone my age celebrates every festival the same way" | Regional, family and generational differences are large |
