## The question

> "How old is Chinese civilisation? I keep reading '5,000 years of continuous history' — is that true?"

## Replaying the framework

**Step 1, classify.** Not a lookup. It is **Premise check** (the "5,000 years / continuous / recorded" framing bundles several assumptions) + **Genesis** (when does written history start?) + **Language** (what does "continuous" mean here?).

**Step 2, lenses.** Time & variation (separate culture, writing, statehood), Language & symbols (what 朝 / 代 / 文明 actually denote), Material & technology (what counts as evidence: oracle bones, bronzes, excavation).

**Step 3, evidence.** The Step 0a search gate must pass — if it fails, use the NO-SEARCH CONTRACT and stop. Search is mandatory for every date here. `anchors.md` supplies the chronology skeleton and the query terms, **not** the answer: verified against retrieved sources, Shang oracle bones c. 1600–1046 BCE are the earliest mature writing; earlier cultures and traditionally dated Xia remain a mix of later texts and ongoing archaeology. A number like "5,000 years" refers to a civilisational narrative, not 5,000 years of written records.

**Step 4, generate.** Model shape:

> Short answer: roughly 3,000+ years of written records, with older archaeological cultures before them. "5,000 years" is a cultural shorthand, not a documentary claim.
>
> **[Time & variation]** Separate three clocks: settled agricultural cultures, the first mature writing system, and the first unified state (Qin, 221 BCE). They begin at different times. Also separate continuity of **script and cultural practices** from continuity of any single state — dynasties rose and fell.
>
> **[Material]** What we actually have: oracle bones, bronze inscriptions, excavated settlements, later transmitted texts. The Xia is traditionally dated but still discussed in relation to archaeology; say "traditionally dated" rather than giving a firm start year.
>
> **[Language]** 朝 (cháo, dynasty) is a historian's filing system. Ordinary food, clothing and dialects did not change at a dynasty boundary. 汉字 (Hànzì) carries the name of the Han not because writing began then, but because that era standardised it.
>
> **[Bridge]** This is closer to how people talk about "ancient Greece" or "the Roman world" than to a single unbroken institution.
>
> **[Uncertainty]** Dates before c. 841 BCE are approximate; the relationship between the Xia and specific excavated cultures is still researched. Verify with a museum or archaeology institution before quoting a figure.
>
> **[Closers]** Search `甲骨文`, `夏商周断代`, `朝代 科普`. Today: read one museum post on oracle bones and note what the object itself proves.

## What this case demonstrates

- A question that looks like "give me the timeline" is really a **premise check**.
- The answer is built from lenses plus a verified orientation, not from a pre-written "history of China" section.
- The most valuable move is **separating clocks** — culture, writing, state — which is a general operator, reusable for "how old is the Great Wall?", "is Hanfu ancient?", "when did the Silk Road open?".
