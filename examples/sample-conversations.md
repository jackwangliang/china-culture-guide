# Sample Conversations — operational domain

Six model interactions plus one out-of-scope example, all from the **operational domain** (search, groups, accounts, learning plans). They show the expected shape of an answer: short answer → numbered steps → something copy-pasteable → a recency/safety note → one next action.

For the **cultural domain** — origins, meaning, objects, comparison, premise checks and the no-search gate — see [`culture-decoding-examples.md`](culture-decoding-examples.md) and the full worked case in `references/cases/stir-fry.md`. Cultural questions require live web search; the operational examples below work offline.

---

## 1. "Why does everyone insist on paying the whole bill? It makes me uncomfortable."

**Load:** `02-customs-and-daily-life.md` (a worked customs case). If the user goes on to ask *why this custom exists historically*, switch to `framework.md` and the four-step cultural workflow instead of staying in the operational domain.

**Good answer shape:**

> It is a warmth signal, not a test. In many Chinese settings, offering to pay shows that the host values the relationship (人情 / rénqíng, reciprocity); refusing once or twice is part of the same polite exchange. Among younger friends in big cities, splitting the bill (AA 制 / AA zhì) is also common, so asking is fine.
>
> 1. Offer to pay once, sincerely.
> 2. If they insist, thank them warmly and let them pay.
> 3. Offer to treat next time — and actually do it.
>
> Say: 谢谢，下次我请客！/ xièxie, xià cì wǒ qǐngkè! ("Thanks, next time it's my treat!")
>
> Customs vary by region, generation and the relationship; this is not a rule everyone follows.
>
> **Today:** learn that one phrase and use it the next time someone treats you.

**Why it works:** explains the logic, avoids "Chinese people always", gives one usable phrase, and ends with an action.

## 2. "I have zero Chinese and 20 minutes a day. What exactly do I do for four weeks?"

**Load:** `03-learning-chinese.md` (+ `09-phrasebook.md` for the intro line)

**Good answer shape:**

> Start with speaking and listening, not characters. Mandarin has tones and characters, but no verb conjugation, gender or plural endings, so the hardest part is front-loaded.
>
> 1. Install **HelloChinese** (one lesson a day) and **Pleco** (dictionary). Just two apps for now.
> 2. Daily 20 minutes: 5 min tone drills, 10 min one lesson spoken out loud, 3 min three characters via radicals, 2 min one real word you saw that day.
> 3. Week 3: add one real post a day from Xiaohongshu, write down three guessable words.
> 4. Week 4: send a short self-introduction to a language partner.
>
> Copy: 你好，我是初学者，请多指教。/ nǐ hǎo, wǒ shì chūxuézhě, qǐng duō zhǐjiào.
>
> App features and pricing change; check the current store listing. HSK levels have been revised — use chinesetest.cn if exams become a goal.
>
> **Today:** finish lesson one out loud and type 你好 with a pinyin keyboard.

**Why it works:** three tools maximum, a timed routine, a milestone, no exam pressure.

## 3. "How do I find WeChat articles about tea? I can't read Chinese menus, let alone a search bar."

**Load:** `05-wechat-search-and-accounts.md` (+ `08-getting-set-up.md` glossary)

**Good answer shape:**

> 搜一搜 / sōu yī sōu is WeChat's own search engine and indexes a lot of Chinese long-form writing that web search misses. You only need two or three Chinese characters to start.
>
> 1. On the WeChat home screen, tap the search field, or go 发现 / fāxiàn ("Discover") → 搜一搜.
> 2. Type `茶道 入门` ("tea ceremony, beginner") or `茶叶 推荐` ("tea recommendations").
> 3. Tap the 文章 / wénzhāng ("Articles") tab to filter; use 公众号 / gōngzhònghào to find accounts to follow.
> 4. Open a result, long-press text → 翻译 / fānyì ("Translate") if available, and 收藏 / shōucáng to save it.
> 5. Follow one account whose last three posts look useful; 星标 / xīngbiāo ("Star") it.
>
> Keyword recipe: `topic noun + intent word`, e.g. 入门 (intro), 教程 (how-to), 推荐 (recommendations), 科普 (explainer).
>
> Menu names and translation coverage differ by WeChat version and account region — follow the on-screen wording and verify in-app.
>
> **Today:** search `茶道 入门`, save two articles, and write down the three characters you see most often.

**Why it works:** real entry points, real result tabs, a reusable keyword formula, no invented account names.

## 4. "How do I actually join a QQ group for language exchange?"

**Load:** `06-qq-groups.md` (+ `09-phrasebook.md`)

**Good answer shape:**

> QQ has public group search, which WeChat does not — that is why it is the easiest place to find people to talk to.
>
> 1. Register QQ with a mobile number (an international number usually works for SMS; verify in-app).
> 2. 联系人 / liánxìrén ("Contacts") → 找群 / zhǎo qún ("Find Groups").
> 3. Search `语伴`, `中英互换` or `英语角`, read the 群公告 / qún gōnggào ("group announcement") and member activity before applying.
> 4. Tap 申请加入 / shēnqǐng jiārù ("Apply to join") and write a short 验证信息 / yànzhèng xìnxī ("verification message"); an admin approves it.
> 5. After joining: read 群规 / qúnguī ("group rules"), set your 群名片 (group nickname), watch for a day, then introduce yourself.
>
> Copy: 你好！我来自法国，正在学中文。希望加入交流学习，谢谢！/ Nǐ hǎo! Wǒ láizì Fǎguó, zhèngzài xué Zhōngwén. Xīwàng jiārù jiāoliú xuéxí, xièxie!
>
> I will not give you a group number — they expire and change. Group QR invites are usually time-limited too. Never pay an entry fee or share ID documents with a group admin.
>
> **Today:** read three group announcements and send **one** tailored application.

**Why it works:** teaches discovery, refuses to fabricate a group number, includes the safety line.

## 5. "I like hot pot and street photography. What should I follow?"

**Load:** `07-interest-topics-and-creators.md`, then `05-wechat-search-and-accounts.md`

**Good answer shape:**

> Start from the two hobbies you already have — that keeps the feed useful. Both map to high-interest themes (food, 78.4% in a 2025 global survey; visual culture and everyday life are strong on photo platforms).
>
> 1. Turn each hobby into short Chinese nouns: 火锅 (hot pot), 街拍 (street photography).
> 2. Add intent words: `火锅 攻略`, `家常菜 教程`, `街拍 技巧`, `摄影 入门`.
> 3. Search each in 搜一搜 → 文章 and on Xiaohongshu or Bilibili.
> 4. Judge accounts by 原创 (original posts), steady cadence, real comments, and how much is pure product selling.
> 5. Follow two, not twenty. Use 收藏 and 点赞 to train the feed, and 不感兴趣 to prune it.
>
> Comment template: 这张照片拍得真好！请问这是在哪里？/ zhè zhāng zhàopiàn pāi de zhēn hǎo! qǐngwèn zhè shì zài nǎlǐ?
>
> I am giving you keywords, not account names — creators change, and a made-up handle is worse than no recommendation.
>
> **Today:** one 15-minute session — search, save three posts, follow two accounts, leave one specific comment.

**Why it works:** personalises rather than dumping the top-ten list; no invented creators.

## 6. "What are the ten things people outside China are most curious about? Nothing political, please."

**Load:** `07-interest-topics-and-creators.md`

**Good answer shape:**

> A 2025 CCTV / Renmin University survey of overseas respondents gives a useful backbone: Chinese food 78.4%, traditional medicine 70.2%, festivals 67.5%, traditional arts 67.1%, martial arts 64.2%, film and TV 63.7%, the Chinese language 60.1%; 77.2% followed Chinese technology or its applications. Inbound-travel reporting adds the Great Wall (42.98% recognition), pandas (34.72%) and a surge in visa-free visits, while the 2025 RedNote migration made everyday friendship and language exchange a major theme.
>
> 1. Food and dining → 家常菜 教程
> 2. Everyday tech and convenience → 移动支付 攻略
> 3. Wellness and seasonal habits → 八段锦 教程
> 4. Festivals and customs → 红包 来历
> 5. Tea, arts and aesthetics → 茶道 入门
> 6. Kung fu and Tai Chi → 太极 入门
> 7. Film, TV and games → 国漫 推荐
> 8. Learning Mandarin → 学中文 方法
> 9. History and heritage → 朝代 科普
> 10. Making friends and online communities → 语伴
>
> These figures come from different surveys with different populations and dates, so treat the order as a guide, not a precise ranking. Every theme here is non-political and non-military by design.
>
> **Today:** tell me which one overlaps a hobby you already have, and I will build a 15-minute search plan for it.

**Why it works:** gives data with attribution and caveats, pairs every theme with a keyword, ends by personalising.
